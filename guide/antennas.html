MC2-D: three external antennas

MC2-D is the current 100 × 100 mm, two-copper-layer carrier for finished modules. It retains the local Thread Border Router, Ember Zigbee, active Bluetooth proxy, Z-Wave transport, CC1101 RX/TX, LD2450 and BME680. Its change is three independent external 2.4 GHz radio paths. This is an engineering prototype: builds and CAD checks do not demonstrate house coverage or physical fit.

Start with the visual guide and pin lookup, native hardware, firmware instructions, and full-hub printable enclosure.

Exact module selection

  • U1: Espressif ESP32-DevKitC-VIE / ESP32-WROVER-IE. Classic ESP32 ECOv3, factory external RF connector, 8 MB flash and 8 MB physical PSRAM; two 19-pin rows. The application maps 4 MB PSRAM for capability-based allocation at quad 40 MHz. It runs Ethernet, the BLE 4.2 proxy and local Thread Border Router. Do not substitute WROOM (no PSRAM), VE (onboard antenna), an older WROVER revision, or the former S3 board. MC2-A and MC2-D footprints and binaries differ.
  • U2: Seeed XIAO ESP32C6 (Pre-Soldered), SKU 102010636. Four MB flash, native USB and factory 2×7 male headers. UART RX17/TX16 are on D7/D6. The factory GPIO8 pull-up replaces the old carrier R3. Recovery uses two flexible insulated leads, TP8/EN → J10.1 and TP2/BOOT (GPIO9) → J10.2. Those are underside pads, not header pins; the carrier has an access window. The matching firmware selects external RF with GPIO3 LOW, GPIO14 HIGH before Thread starts. Do not flash the old TinyC6 I²C-expander image.
  • U3: Seeed XIAO MG24. Retain the same Ember protocol family, with a separately built external-antenna firmware. PB04=1 and PB05=1 select its U.FL path before radio initialization. Its onboard antenna and external path are alternatives, not two simultaneous radiators.
  • U4: WIZnet WIZ850io. Retain the SPI Ethernet module. Its RJ45 is a data port; the separate standards-compliant PoE splitter supplies the low-voltage circuit.

Module sources: Espressif ordering and pin guide, Seeed XIAO ESP32C6 pin map and source files, Seeed antenna selection.

Three antenna paths

Buy three TE Connectivity/Linx ANT-2.4-LCW-SMA antennas and three Taoglas CAB.618.E pigtails. Each 150 mm cable has an I-PEX MHF I/U.FL-compatible right-angle radio plug and a standard SMA female bulkhead. Each antenna has the matching standard SMA male plug, 50 Ω impedance, 2400–2485 MHz specification and 2.8 dBi peak gain. These are not RP-SMA connectors. Confirm center contacts and the exact connector family; MHF III/4 miniature plugs are not interchangeable.

  1. WROVER-IE U.FL → its cable → BLE bulkhead → antenna.
  2. XIAO ESP32C6 switched U.FL → its cable → THREAD bulkhead → antenna.
  3. XIAO MG24 U.FL → its cable → ZIGBEE bulkhead → antenna.

No RF combiner is used. Mate miniature connectors squarely with power removed; secure the cable away from the connector and leave slack. Connect the antennas before operating the radios. Inspect the physical cable route before final assembly. The enclosure's 65 mm adjacent bulkhead pitch is a starting layout, not a measured isolation rating. The 150 mm cable selection still needs a physical dry fit; do not pull it taut to reach a bulkhead. Use the FH1-MC2-D-PWR1 print package: its round-hole coupon matches the 3 mm wall, and the revised routes reserve a 6 mm cable-length shortfall allowance plus 25 mm service slack. That shortfall is an engineering budget, not a manufacturer-guaranteed tolerance. See the pigtail selection.

Primary specifications: TE antenna, Taoglas 150 mm assembly.

Pin connections and initial programming

Use the generated connector ledger and schematic, rather than MC1 pin numbers. Net names such as THREAD_RX and ZB_RX describe the radio end: therefore the host transmitter connects to that RX net.

  • ESP32 GPIO26/TX → C6 GPIO17/RX, U2 pad 8/D7; C6 GPIO16/TX, U2 pad 7/D6 → ESP32 GPIO36/RX.
  • ESP32 GPIO27 → J10.1 → C6 bottom TP8/EN; ESP32 GPIO15 → J10.2 → C6 bottom TP2/BOOT9. Recovery outputs are open-drain. Use 30 AWG flexible insulated leads; secure a slack loop before the joints. Read the bottom-view drawing before soldering, and inspect through the carrier service window.
  • ESP32 GPIO32/TX → MG24 D7/PC07 RX; MG24 D6/PC06 TX → ESP32 GPIO39/RX. ESP32 GPIO33/RTS → MG24 D4/PC04 CTS; MG24 D5/PC05 RTS → ESP32 GPIO14/CTS.
  • LD2450 TX → ESP32 GPIO18/RX; ESP32 GPIO23/TX → LD2450 RX.
  • I²C uses GPIO21/SDA and GPIO22/SCL with 3.3 V logic. Retain the documented WK2132 bridge and BME680 address/pull-up configuration; never add 5 V pull-ups.
  • Shared SPI: GPIO12/SCK, GPIO2/MOSI, GPIO19/MISO. WIZ850io uses GPIO5/CS, GPIO34/IRQ and GPIO4/RESET; CC1101 uses GPIO13/CS, GPIO25/GDO0 TX and GPIO35/GDO2 RX. Do not use other breakout boards with unknown strap pull-ups.

GPIO6–11 belong to flash, GPIO16/17 to PSRAM, GPIO1/3 to the board's USB-UART bridge, and GPIO0 to ROM download entry. GPIO34/35/36/39 are input-only and have no internal pulls. Fit the carrier's 10 kΩ pull-downs on GPIO12 and GPIO2: GPIO12 must be low at reset for the selected module's 3.3 V flash supply; GPIO2 must be low for UART download mode. They connect only to SPI peripheral inputs. Preserve the documented chip-select and C6 boot pull-ups and the C6 factory GPIO8 pull-up. R3 is not fitted on MC2-D. Never apply an eFuse workaround to an unknown module.

The UART0 logger is disabled and the IDF application console is off so UART0 can serve the radar after boot. Zigbee uses UART1, Thread uses UART2. USB-UART remains available for ROM programming on GPIO1/3, but is not a running application log port. The firmware locks the supported silicon to ECOv3 or newer.

Program and verify each controller while loose, using the MC2-D classic-ESP32 factory image, C6 RCP and external MG24 NCP/bootloader. XIAO has its own GPIO8 pull-up; use its BOOT/RESET buttons for initial loose-board USB download. No temporary TinyC6 pull-up is needed. Keep USB disconnected during assembled PoE operation. Neither an MC1 nor MC2-A S3 OTA image is compatible with this processor. Flash the new host over its Micro-B USB-UART connection first, under the new mc2-classic-hub identity. Do not reuse an S3 factory image or its boot offset. Keep physical recovery possible before soldering. All carrier components face the same side; solder low axial parts and headers first, then modules from the back. Check pad numbers, diode cathodes, continuity, supply polarity and clearance before applying power. The C6 and MG24 headers arrive pre-soldered; their tails still need soldering to the carrier. Fit and inspect the two C6 recovery leads before final assembly. No RF resistor relocation is specified.

The firmware guide and network update instructions provide reproducible commands and reject the wrong radio target/antenna mode. Keep one deployed node with the configured identity. Back up Thread credentials, Zigbee state and HA configuration before a future physical migration.

PoE and retained equipment

Power remains battery-fed PoE switch → Ethernet cable → isolated PoE splitter → regulated/fused low-voltage rails. The splitter's data output feeds WIZ850io; its DC output feeds the documented regulators. No direct battery connection to this carrier, new injector or raw PoE-voltage input is introduced. Check the actual splitter rating, regulator headroom and switch port budget under all radios' simultaneous load.

Retain the WK2132/DFR0627 UART bridge, existing regional ZAC93, band-matched 433.92 MHz CC1101 and antenna, LD2450, BME680, power converters, fuses and harnesses. Their exact carrier endpoints are in the connector ledger. ZAC93 retains its stock antenna; LD2450 retains its 24 GHz printed array. Neither belongs on a 2.4 GHz SMA antenna. Locate BME680 away from the converters and warm electronics, and keep metal and wires out of the radar's active face.

The FH1 full-hub housing has a nominal 215 × 215 × 78 mm main shell. Individual removable trays retain the isolated splitter, buck, DFR0627, ZAC93 and CC1101; a utility tray secures the insulated DC adapter and fuse. The LD2450 sits behind the room-facing lid window, and the BME680 has a ventilated pod attached outside the main shell with an air gap. The pod and external antennas increase the installed envelope. Bulkheads and coax stay on the base when the lid is removed; disconnect the radar harness before removing it fully. See the complete assembly and ZAC93 pin map and mechanical package for exact placement, print files, mount hardware and the limits of the digital fit checks. The selected finished splitter is Waveshare POE-SPLITTER-25W-5.5-2.1; the historical Silvertel EvalPoE assembly is not a substitute within this layout. Print the fit coupon first and verify delivered module heights, mated plugs, solder tails, cable bends and attachment strength. The earlier carrier-only shell is retained as a historical option.

Bluetooth compatibility and coverage

The classic ESP32 supports BLE 4.2 legacy advertisements and active connections. The configured three-connection HA proxy is retained, but BLE 5 extended advertisements and LE Coded PHY are unavailable. External antennas do not add those features. Confirm any sensor requiring them before changing the host.

External antennas move the radiators away from the carrier ground planes, Ethernet metalwork and converters. They improve placement options; they do not guarantee coverage of 1,000 ft² / about 93 m². Layout, walls, interference, end-device transmit power and installed mesh routers still matter. BLE sensors that are not mesh devices do not gain a Thread/Zigbee mesh route.

Follow the RF commissioning procedure, including weak-room measurements, simultaneous traffic and at least a 72-hour soak. The results sheet deliberately starts with not_run. If coverage is inadequate, first adjust placement and antenna orientation, then add appropriate powered Zigbee/Thread routers or a second Ethernet BLE proxy. Do not compensate with arbitrary transmit-power increases. Hardware operation, measured energy use and regulatory compliance remain separate from compilation and CAD validation.

The host migration record explains the exact board choice, memory budget and limitations.

The historical MC1 guide remains available for existing boards. Its Nano/H2 pin map, compact case and onboard MG24 image are not MC2 parts.

MC2-D is the active classic ESP32 design. Physical fit, concurrent operation and coverage still require your measurements.
