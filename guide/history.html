One current design, retained engineering history

On 19 October 2026 MC2-C replaced the obsolete/unavailable original TinyC6-U-01 with TinyC6[D] / TC6D-01, while retaining the classic ESP32 host and the other services. The new board's external antenna requires an I²C-controlled switch; the matching RCP firmware performs and verifies that selection before starting Thread. The carrier and FH1 geometry, pin contracts, shopping list and fabrication package were updated together. The MC2-B source snapshot preserves the previous fixed-U.FL build. Use the current ordering record for new boards.

On 18 October 2026 MC2-B replaced the S3 host with the previous-generation ESP32-DevKitC-VIE / WROVER-IE to reduce the host purchase cost. Its new 38-pin footprint, pin map, boot-strap resistors and classic-ESP32 binary are not interchangeable with MC2-A. It retains the C6/MG24 radio paths and application services; BLE is limited to the original ESP32's 4.2 feature set. See the migration record.

Earlier on 18 October 2026 MC2-A introduced a 100 × 100 mm carrier with factory external-antenna S3/C6 modules and external-mode XIAO MG24 firmware. It retained local services while separating all three 2.4 GHz antenna feeds. The MC2-A source snapshot preserves its S3 CAD, firmware and appearance concepts. MC1 remains historical and continues to have regression checks. Its last 47.5 × 75 mm layout, Nano 16 MB flash target and H2 pin map are not MC2.

On 17 October 2026 the project chose MC1: one carrier for finished modules. The decision prioritizes existing functions and Home Assistant compatibility over using identical ESP32-H2 processors everywhere. Factory male module pins are soldered directly into plated carrier holes; female module sockets are excluded.

The design registry identifies the only active custom PCB design. The current assembly guide and current visual guide are the starting points.

Why the alternatives are retained

The first MC1 layout used a 160 × 100 mm outline, followed by a 100 × 90 mm full-size S3 carrier, then an 80 × 90 mm Nano carrier with USB cable corridors. The subsequent 65 × 75 mm Nano carrier removed those corridors, retained physical USB shells and implemented preassembly programming plus network updates. It introduced H2 RX23/TX24 and the documented R7 removal so the standard ROM loader is reachable, while retaining shared SPI and independent radio controllers.

The historical 51 × 75 mm two-sided carrier placed S3/H2/MG24 toward the room and WIZ850io, male harness headers and five axial parts toward the ceiling. Its electrical pin assignments and firmware are unchanged from the 65 × 75 mm revision. Staged solder access and measured opposing-tail clearance are now explicit assembly requirements. Previous snapshots are historical; use the current front and back drawings together.

The previous C0/M0, I0 and D0 sources, measurements, build proofs and renders remain available to reproduce earlier work. They are historical references, not additional shopping lists or alternative current MC1 builds. Replacing an old board image with an MC1 image under the old filename would misrepresent its source and provenance.

  • Original modular assembly: Raspberry Pi, internal Ethernet switch and two Ethernet ESP controllers. Its enclosure and wiring are not MC1 geometry.
  • LP-P0: the earlier S3 software/electrical baseline, retained for regression testing and shared component implementation.
  • C0 + M0: carrier plus a separately manufactured MG24 adapter.
  • I0: integrated radio board with its own power, Ethernet and support circuits.
  • D0: home-etched carrier with wire links and adapter boards.
  • H2 Quartet: a useful feasibility experiment, rejected as the compatibility baseline. It introduced an unimplemented H2 local border router, different Zigbee backup/integration behavior and a new interprocessor transport.

Reading images and claims

MC1 CAD renders are generated from its own PCB sources. Assembly illustrations are labeled separately from CAD and from photographs. No rendering establishes that a physical board has been assembled, tested, certified or measured.

The H2 function audit records why making all processors identical would not currently be a function-preserving simplification. The earlier hardware has not been physically qualified either; its compiled firmware and documented interfaces are the comparison baseline, not field-test evidence.

An intermediate mounting revision added a 6 × 27 mm ear for a fourth support beside RJ45 (57 × 75 mm overall). Two lower supports straddled its opening. Axial parts may use arbitrary angles where the final routed result improves; all functions and firmware pin assignments remain unchanged. See the mounting and routing review.

The following four-hole revision returned to a 51 × 75 mm rectangle: WIZ moves 6 mm toward the center, XIAO rotates, and a support fits between U2 and U3. It keeps four mounting points without the ear. The required assembly order is now explicitly the complete back first, then the front. Rear-first solder access and unchanged electrical connectivity are checked separately from physical height and insertion-force qualification. The former ear layout is preserved under the mounting study.

Single-anchor split-rail revision

The final MC1 nano-single-anchor-rails revision is 47.5 × 75 mm. It removes H1, H2 and H4, retains H3 between U2 and U3, and uses split enclosure rails for edge support. The PCB is 6.86% smaller than the previous four-hole 51 × 75 mm rectangle. S3 and H2 move closer together; narrow clear edge lands remain for the enclosure lips. All back components are still soldered before the front. No sockets, new electronics, pin-map or firmware changes are introduced.

The preceding four-hole native board is immutable comparison evidence. The mounting study defines the split support interface and fit coupons. The carrier enclosure prototype extends that interface into printable housing parts. It does not house the external power, UART bridge, sensors and other radio modules. Actual module and cable fit, printed tolerances, RF behavior and load capacity remain unqualified.

MC2-C is the active classic ESP32 design. Physical fit, concurrent operation and coverage still require your measurements.
