Start here: build the MC2-D / FH1 hub

Use this route for the MC2-D carrier and FH1 full-hub enclosure. It takes you from loose parts to a tested Home Assistant node. The hardware is an engineering prototype: software and CAD checks have passed, but the physical assembly, thermal behaviour and house coverage still need measurement.

For a readable local guide, run python3 tools/preview.py from the repository root and open the printed URL. It serves documentation; it does not flash boards or connect to Home Assistant. Stop it with Ctrl+C when finished.

1. Identify the parts before ordering or soldering

Start with the shopping list, carrier BOM and full-case inventory. Those cover different parts of the same build; the carrier BOM alone is not a complete hub.

Check the labels on the four carrier modules:

  • U1: ESP32-DevKitC-VIE / WROVER-IE, classic ESP32 with two 19-pin rows, external antenna, 8 MB flash and 8 MB physical PSRAM. Already ordered by the owner.
  • U2: Seeed XIAO ESP32C6 (Pre-Soldered), 102010636, the 4 MB board with factory 2×7 male headers. Use MC2-D RCP firmware with GPIO3/14 external antenna control. Its underside EN/BOOT pads need two recovery leads to J10.
  • U3: Seeed XIAO MG24, pre-soldered SKU 102010690, for external-antenna Zigbee.
  • U4: WIZ850io, the data-only SPI Ethernet module.

The off-carrier parts include the isolated 5 V PoE splitter, RECOM R-78K3.3-1.0 3.3 V buck with its 27 µF input capacitor and perfboard mount, DFR0627 UART bridge, regional Zooz ZAC93, CC1101 and its 433.92 MHz antenna, LD2450 and BME680. The ZAC93 is already received for this project; do not buy a second one by default. Each of the three 2.4 GHz radios needs its own specified antenna and pigtail. Check actual dimensions, connectors and quantities against the assembly inventory.

Ready to continue: the received parts match the active revision, and every module, cable and antenna has an identified place in the inventory. Keep MC1, MC2-A/S3 and Pi-era instructions in the history section.

2. Program the three controllers while they are loose

Follow the MC2-D firmware guide for tool versions, commands and board selection. Attach the appropriate antenna before a radio runs. Use USB power only for each loose board.

  1. Prepare a private deployment directory and replace its generated API, OTA and MQTT credentials, broker and RF topics with your intended values. The validation build has disposable credentials and build-only/rf/* topics; it is not a deployable household configuration.
  2. Give U1/VIE a full initial USB-UART flash, including its MC2-D partition table. The node name is mc2-classic-hub. An S3 or MC1 image, and an app-only OTA over an old partition layout, are not valid initial installations.
  3. Build and flash the XIAO ESP32C6 RCP with the matching pinned ESP-IDF. Use the factory BOOT/RESET buttons for loose-board USB download. The board already includes the GPIO8 pull-up. Verify the MC2-D XIAO identity and external-antenna selection; TinyC6 binaries are incompatible.
  4. Follow the MC2-D XIAO MG24 programming guide, which selects --antenna external, and program its external initial HEX through the XIAO's onboard CMSIS-DAP probe. Output filenames retain mc1 because the build recipe is shared; the guide gives the current MC2-D host connections.
  5. Leave the ZAC93 on its Zooz firmware. It is not an ESPHome target.

Ready to continue: programming and verification succeed for all three selected boards; keep the exact private configuration, image hashes and build reports. Loose-module programming does not yet verify Ethernet or the radios working together. Unplug USB before assembling the PoE-powered hub.

3. Print small parts, dry-fit, then assemble the carrier

Use the FH1 print instructions and exploded layout. Print the fit coupon and ZAC93 tray first, then check the real board and its underside mating socket. The largest part is 215 × 215 mm; the identified TRONXY XY-3 has a nominal 310 × 310 mm bed. Check the slicer's usable area and brim before printing.

Dry-fit every received component, mated plug, solder tail and cable route. Follow the MC2-D assembly order: all modules face the same side, low axial parts and carrier headers go first. The C6/MG24 modules use factory headers. Fit and inspect C6 underside EN/BOOT leads above the carrier, accessible through the service window with the carrier removed from the case; module tails are soldered from the back. The historical MC1 rear-first assembly is a different carrier. Install each off-board module's restraint, the radar holder and the ventilated BME680 pod; do not rely on cable friction to hold boards.

Ready to continue: no board or plug is forced into position, miniature coax plugs have slack, the ZAC93 antenna area is clear, the radar faces its window, and the lid closes without compressing a wire. A rendered fit is not this test.

4. Wire and test the power before connecting Home Assistant

Keep the full wiring image beside the pin-by-pin assembly instructions. Read actual pad numbers and module labels; drawings and cable colours do not define the connector's mating-side orientation.

  • The power path is existing battery-fed PoE switch → isolated 5 V splitter → fuse/J1 → buck/J2 and controller rails. The splitter's data-only RJ45 plugs into WIZ850io. Raw PoE does not connect to the carrier.
  • The RECOM replaces the earlier Pololu at the same J2 interface. Use the new PWR1 power tray and power wiring, including both ground returns and correct capacitor polarity. Its input must remain at least 4.5 V under load; take 5 V before the controller isolation diodes.
  • With power disconnected, continuity-check J1–J7 and J10 (TP8/EN and TP2/BOOT) and look for shorts between 5 V, 3.3 V and ground. Preserve the specified boot pull-ups/pull-downs and keep controller-local 3.3 V outputs separate from the peripheral buck rail.
  • Meter the disconnected splitter and buck first. Then connect the electronics and check the rails again under load, including at ZAC93. Disconnect power before changing any wiring.
  • ZAC93 uses a remapped cable: J4.1→pin 1, J4.2→pin 6, J4.3→pin 9, J4.4→pin 8/RX, J4.5→pin 10/TX. It is not a straight-through 2×5 cable. Its DFR0627 bridge is on J3; set IA1=1, IA0=0 and use UART1 at 115200 8N1 with register/FIFO addresses 0x50/0x51. Reserve 0x50–0x57 for the bridge; BME680 remains 0x77. The former 1/1 setting overlaps BME680 and must not be used.

Ready to continue: measured rails and polarity agree with the component requirements, no component overheats, Ethernet links, and the router gives the hub an address. Record that address; use it if .local names do not resolve.

5. Bring up one service at a time

Use Settings → Devices & services → Add integration in Home Assistant. Keep the lid open for the first checks. Replace HUB_IP below with the measured hub address; the placeholder is not a real hostname.

  1. ESPHome: add mc2-classic-hub (or HUB_IP) with the private encrypted API key. It uses TCP 6053. Confirm Hub Uptime advances and inspect the health entities. Read running logs through the encrypted API; the USB-UART application console is disabled. Verify a real LD2450 movement event and fresh BME680 readings (default update interval 60 s). Check a compatible Bluetooth sensor through its HA integration; add an active-connection test if your devices use GATT.
  2. Zigbee: give the coordinator exactly one owner. For ZHA choose the Silicon Labs EZSP/Ember radio with socket://HUB_IP:6638 and 115200 baud. For Zigbee2MQTT instead use tcp://HUB_IP:6638, adapter: ember and 115200 baud. The firmware supplies physical RTS/CTS. Confirm the NCP is identified and a spare test device reports and responds.
  3. Thread: add OpenThread Border Router at http://HUB_IP:8081. OTBR runs on this hub; it does not require another Linux border router. ready_uncommissioned_or_disabled can be normal before a network is set up; after joining/creating the intended network, verify Thread state and an actual device. Use the HA Thread procedure to check the chosen dataset and phone credentials. A matching network name alone does not mean matching credentials.
  4. Matter-over-Thread: use the HA Matter integration/server and Companion app with the device's setup code. The hub supplies Thread transport, not a Matter controller. The phone's Bluetooth, IPv6 and multicast connectivity still matter; this hub's BLE proxy does not replace phone commissioning. Follow HA's Matter setup and the Thread device guide, then test a real command.
  5. Z-Wave: configure Z-Wave JS / Z-Wave JS UI with the native ESPHome transport from the ZAC93 instructions. It uses 6053 plus the URL-encoded API key. The HA Z-Wave integration connects to that Z-Wave JS server's WebSocket, not directly to the hub. Confirm ZAC93 is identified in the correct region and test a device. Use one Z-Wave driver only; port 6638 is Zigbee.
  6. 433 MHz RF: confirm the private MQTT broker and topic contract, then receive a known remote event. Test transmission against an intended test receiver and verify reception resumes. Compiling successfully does not establish that learned codes or the household's MQTT topics were retained.

Ready to continue: each intended service has passed a physical event or command test. A connected TCP socket or visible HA integration alone is not end-to-end evidence. See HA's external OTBR configuration for the REST endpoint and Bluetooth integrations for supported remote adapters.

6. Close the case, qualify, then migrate

Repeat the same tests with the case closed and at its final position. Follow the RF commissioning procedure, including simultaneous traffic, intended active BLE connections, measured heap/temperature/supply behaviour, recovery after PoE loss and the 72-hour soak. Record results in the coverage sheet.

Before switching an existing home, save HA configuration, private firmware secrets, Thread credentials and Zigbee/Z-Wave backups. Keep the old coordinator configuration available. Move one service at a time, stop its previous driver, and verify the same test events before moving the next service. Do not erase radio state or re-include devices simply to change a host connection.

Rollback: stop the new service's driver before restoring the previous connection and its appropriate backup. Do not run two owners of one Zigbee or Z-Wave radio. A moved ZAC93 keeps its network state unless reset; return that same radio to its old host if needed. A later firmware rollback requires a known-good image for this exact hardware and partition layout; use the checked network update and recovery guide.

Troubleshooting: symptom → first checks

  • No DHCP address / HA cannot reach the hub: check PoE switch power, measured 5 V/3.3 V, the splitter's data output into U4 and Ethernet link. Try its DHCP address instead of .local. An address proves neither ESPHome credentials nor Thread's IPv6 path; test those separately.
  • USB connects but no running logs appear: expected after boot. UART0 serves the radar and console output is disabled. Use encrypted ESPHome API logs; USB-UART is retained for initial flashing and recovery.
  • Host fails to boot or reports missing PSRAM: check the exact VIE/WROVER-IE board and factory image, then inspect the GPIO12/GPIO2 boot pull-downs and supply integrity. Do not change eFuses to disguise a wrong module or strap.
  • Thread says quarantined or maintenance: read Thread Last Fault and C6 UART Update State. Check C6 image, power, UART wiring and boot straps. Complete an interrupted update if necessary, then use Retry Quarantined Thread after correcting the cause. Do not erase the host's Thread dataset.
  • Zigbee times out: close any second ZHA/Zigbee2MQTT client. Check TCP 6638, all four UART/RTS/CTS wires, the external-antenna MG24 image and open J8 during normal operation. Poor range also warrants checking the U.FL path and antenna.
  • ZAC93 is missing or stops responding: check the J4 remap, 3.3 V at ZAC93, bridge address jumpers, UART1 and the API key used by Z-Wave JS. A wk2132_uart log saying UART stream invalid requires a device restart after its cause is corrected; reconnection alone does not reset the bridge driver. A host restart is not a ZAC93 power cycle. If the radio needs a power cycle, stop its driver and cycle PoE with USB disconnected; do not factory reset.
  • BME680 absent / temperature reads high: check J7, the default 0x77 address (or the private bme680_address override for 0x76), 3.3 V pull-ups and the shared bus harness. Keep the sensor opening clear in its external pod and compare after thermal equilibrium. Gas resistance is not calibrated CO₂.
  • LD2450 has no updates: check J6 power and TX→RX crossing at 256000 baud. Compare with the lid open to separate a serial fault from window/placement effects; keep metal and wires away from the radar face.
  • RF events reach the wrong topic or never reach HA: inspect the private broker credentials, topic substitutions and learned-code overrides first. build-only/rf/* means the proof configuration was not prepared for deployment.
  • Works on the bench, fails with the lid or combined traffic: compare the recorded open/closed-case results; inspect cable compression, antenna spacing, power/temperature and Hub Minimum Free Heap/Hub Largest Heap Block. Follow the RF acceptance workflow rather than assuming gain or enclosure clearance guarantees coverage.
MC2-D is the active classic ESP32 design. Physical fit, concurrent operation and coverage still require your measurements.
