# LP-D0: single-sided, wire-assisted DIY carrier

LP-D0 is a prototype for laser toner transfer and manual drilling. It implements the LP-P0 electrical contract on a 190 × 145 mm low-voltage backplane. The required assembly includes bottom copper **and insulated wire links**. It is not a one-layer redesign of the RF modules themselves, and no assembled D0 has been electrically tested yet.

The board accepts only isolated, regulated 5 V. The PoE front end and Pololu D24V22F3 regulator stay external. Never connect an Ethernet PoE pair or 48 V to this PCB. The nominal design ceiling remains 1 A at the 5 V input; actual startup voltage drop and temperature must be measured.

## What can be made with toner transfer

D0 uses 0.6 mm signal tracks, 1.2 mm supply/return tracks, nominal 0.5 mm copper clearance, and 2 mm through-hole lands. All copper tracks are on B.Cu. KiCad represents through-hole pads with annuli on both outer layers; **only the bottom annuli are fabricated**. There are no plated vias, no hidden top copper traces, and no electrical dependence on hole plating. Through-hole components and T-numbered wire holes are soldered on the copper side. The explicitly identified E-numbered joints instead solder to exposed raised-adapter header pins above the board; they do not add a drilled hole.

C0 is a two-layer, mixed SMT/through-hole carrier; printing only its bottom layer would omit connections. M0 has a fine-pitch MGM240P footprint, ground planes and an antenna keepout: order it from a PCB manufacturer. I0 is a four-layer integrated design and is unsuitable for this home process. Existing enclosure mounting patterns do not match D0.

## Purchased fine-pitch assemblies are still necessary

- U1 is SC16IS752IPW,128 soldered to the **TSSOP side** of an [Adafruit 1208 adapter](https://www.adafruit.com/product/1208), with two 14-pin headers. Looking at the populated TSSOP face, pin 1 is upper-left, pin 14 lower-left, pin 15 lower-right and pin 28 upper-right. Row separation is 15.24 mm and pitch 2.54 mm. The blank adapter does not include the IC or headers.
- U2 is TPS3808G33DBVR on the SOT23-6 side of an [Adafruit 1230 adapter](https://www.adafruit.com/product/1230), with two 3-pin headers. Pin1/2/3 run down the left; 6/5/4 down the right. Row separation 7.62 mm; pitch 2.54 mm. Do not populate the two reverse-side transistor sites. Stock may vary; a different adapter requires matching its pin map and dimensions.
- Keep both adapter PCBs at least 6 mm above D0; some insulated links may pass underneath. Fit and inspect these links before soldering the adapter in place. Use [Adafruit 400 extra-long headers](https://www.adafruit.com/product/400), cut to two 14-pin and two 3-pin lengths (one five-strip pack supplies both adapters). Its [manufacturer drawing](https://cdn-shop.adafruit.com/datasheets/18072.pdf) specifies 0.64 mm square pins, 7.73 mm on each side of a 2.54 mm insulator. Rest the insulator on D0, set the adapter underside 6 mm above D0, solder and trim the long tails. Availability must be checked before ordering.
- The populated [M0 adapter](../carrier/mg24-adapter/README.md) is mandatory for MGM240PA22VNA3. It contains the module decoupling, BOOT and SWD service connections. A bare MGM240P cannot plug into J6.
- S3/H2 development boards, WIZ850io, ZAC93, CC1101, LD2450 and BME680 connect through the named harnesses. This PCB does not invent pin positions for unknown module revisions. Use the verified GPIO/net labels, not guessed connector geometry.

The adapter mating geometry was checked against [Adafruit's published CAD at a fixed commit](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit-SMT-Breakout-PCBs/tree/dbd7a1928c2b668a9edf3d53277063eac4c3e938). U1/U2 still require fine-pitch soldering by the builder or an assembly service. The household-iron process applies to D0 alone.

## Files and assembly

- `diy.kicad_sch`, `diy.kicad_pcb`, `diy.kicad_pro`: native connected assembly schematic and PCB.
- `logical-netlist.json`: original electrical net names and physical part pins.
- `assembly-netlist.json`: bottom-copper islands plus actual off-board wire components W1…Wn.
- `connectors.json`: every connector pin, remote destination and actual board coordinates.
- `assembly-bom.csv`, `harness-bom.csv`: board parts, adapters, mating housings, contacts and materials.
- `jumpers.json`, `jumpers.csv`: every insulated link, AWG, terminal holes, route and cut length. The route is a top-view assembly guide, not copper artwork.
- `drill.csv`: all manually drilled holes in top-view coordinates.
- `print/`: printable transfer, inspection, drilling and assembly documents generated by the dedicated print exporter.

J1–J9 preserve C0's electrical pin numbering. J3 is **not a Raspberry Pi GPIO connector**. J10 changes physically from JST SH to through-hole JST XH, retaining 1=GND / 2=+3V3 / 3=SDA / 4=SCL. Splice to a factory QT cable and continuity-test each conductor. Every cable mating-face view is mirrored relative to the PCB component view; use molded circuit numbers and the square pin 1 land.

For F1 use Littelfuse 022901.5MXP, a 1.5 A slow-blow 2AG cartridge, in the external 01500274Z holder. Adapt the holder's 16 AWG leads to short 22 AWG insulated tails for the 1.1 mm F1 holes; cover the splices and strain-relieve the holder. The manufacturer [catalog](https://www.littelfuse.com/assetdocs/powr-gard-catalog?assetguid=85d36235-a32b-4608-bfed-f9878aa43af0) identifies the 229 series’ 125 VDC rating. The [holder datasheet](https://www.littelfuse.com/assetdocs/fuse-holder-150-datasheet?assetguid=662e5ae4-2c3a-4d7a-8642-e7d9db02703d) specifies 2AG/5×20 compatibility. This does not change D0's **5 V-only** input limit.

Use insulated AWG 24 for power/return jumpers and AWG 26 for signal links. At most two stripped ends share a 1.1 mm terminal hole; trial-fit wire and drill before making the whole board. The cut schedule includes the projected routed length plus 14 mm, with an additional 4 mm for each raised E joint; strip 5 mm at each end and trim the solder tails after assembly. Do not use bare jumpers where paths cross. Keep insulation against the board, avoiding conductive contact with headers, adapter pads or other wires. Support externally connected wires so a pull cannot lift a copper land.

E-numbered endpoints identify a specific U1/U2 header pin, not a new pad. Solder a single wire to its exposed shaft at **4 mm above D0**, before placing the adapter at its required **6 mm underside height**. The first/last 3 mm of that wire passes over the header insulator at the raised height, then descends. Keep the joint compact, insulated from adjacent pins and strain-relieved. Confirm the actual 2 mm space below the daughterboard is sufficient for the joint and wire before final soldering; this fit is not yet physically tested. The board-level adapter courtyards represent the header strips, while assembly drawings retain the full elevated board outlines.

## Firmware and physical acceptance

No GPIO or serial assignment changes. The supplied low-power firmware currently uses 10 MHz W5500 SPI and 4 MHz for the shared CC1101/SC16 bus. D0 has no controlled-impedance routing or ground plane; clock frequencies are not proof that its assembled wires will be reliable. Keep external SPI harnesses at or below an initial 50 mm, retain the interleaved returns in J3, pair each clock/data group with a short return, and measure SPI edges and error behavior with the full load. This starting length is a prototype constraint, not a validated signal-integrity guarantee. The 3 W AC objective is also unmeasured for D0. If a lower SPI rate is required, use an explicit D0 configuration change and repeat the firmware build and throughput tests; do not silently edit the common profile.

Before installing modules: inspect the toner result under magnification; verify no copper bridges; continuity-test all nets against the schematic, including every W link; check 5 V/3.3 V/GND isolation; confirm F1 and regulator polarity. Power from a current-limited 5 V source first, verify 3.3 V at every connector, then add modules in stages. Check U1 crystal startup, its 115200 baud UART, IRQ handling, H2 reset/BOOT, Zigbee RTS/CTS and CC1101 transmit/receive arbitration. Repeat the existing hub acceptance tests with the final enclosure and radios active. Radio antenna spacing and the M0 keepout remain unchanged.

A passed CAD check cannot establish toner adhesion, actual etched track width, hand-drilled annular rings, solder quality, antenna performance, regulatory compliance or production readiness. The board is a workshop prototype, not a validated product for sale.

## Reproduce and validate

Run from the repository root with KiCad 10's Python (which supplies `pcbnew`):

```sh
python3 hardware/low-power/diy/generate.py
python3 -m unittest discover -s hardware/low-power/diy -p 'test_*.py'
python3 hardware/low-power/diy/validate.py --kicad-cli kicad-cli --check-hashes --out /tmp/d0-check
```

`routing-plan.json` is the reviewed geometric input. Regeneration uses it directly and never reruns an autorouter. Optional route-authoring tools are `prepare.py`, `capture_routing.py`, `plan_wires.py` and `complete_wires.py`; they write exploratory files under ignored `.build/`. Their intermediate F.Cu geometry must never be sent to a fabricator or printed for transfer. The final validator calculates copper contact from the actual PCB, joins only the numbered assembly wires, and compares the result to the original pin-level contract. Native ERC/DRC alone is insufficient because an intentionally split island net can be internally connected while the required physical wire is missing.

The released D0 snapshot has 636 bottom tracks, 100 dedicated wire holes, 54 insulated links and one raised joint E1 at U1.11. Native ERC, DRC, schematic parity and unconnected checks report zero violations. The independent graph verifies all 195 component pins across 44 logical nets. These are CAD results, not measurements of a constructed board.
