# Compact S3 study: independent review

**17 October 2026. Study only.** Under the user's priority of a smaller finished module **without losing memory capacity**, prefer developing the **Waveshare S3 Nano-M with direct S3-to-W5500 SPI**, subject to the pin, shared-bus and power checks below. It retains 8 MB PSRAM and increases flash to 16 MB. The LOLIN S3 Mini is a useful lower-change alternative, but its 2 MB PSRAM is a real capacity reduction even if today's application fits. Using the existing H2 or MG24 as an Ethernet intermediary would require substantially more firmware and recovery work. This review does not approve a new carrier for manufacture or assert measured hardware parity. [Nano specification](https://docs.waveshare.com/ESP32-S3-Nano)

## Preferred direction: Nano, with a bounded software change

The Nano exposes 24 GPIO on its two ordinary 15-position header rows. Excluding GPIO0/3/46 leaves 21 application pins, enough when W5500 and CC1101 share SCK/MOSI/MISO while keeping separate chip selects. This does not require a GPIO expander, extra MCU, underside wires or removal of factory parts. It does require a full pin map and shared-SPI lifecycle implementation, rather than assuming the existing two-bus configuration is equivalent. The S3 still runs the host services; H2 stays a Thread RCP and MG24 stays an Ember NCP.

The proposed Nano allocation was independently checked against the manufacturer header schematic: shared SPI SCK/MOSI/MISO **13/11/12**; Ethernet CS/IRQ/reset **14/10/9**; CC1101 CS/GDO0/GDO2 **6/4/5**; I²C SDA/SCL **1/2**; Ember TX/RX/RTS/CTS **17/18/7/21**; Thread TX/RX/reset/BOOT **43/44/47/48**; radar TX/RX **8/38**. These are 21 distinct GPIO, all present on ordinary header pins, with no strap GPIO0/3/46 used. The final generated configuration must retain this same allocation.

The real pin constraints are manageable:

- **GPIO43:** ROM UART output can emit bytes before the application starts. Assigning it to H2 RX is preferable to a chip-select or reset signal. The existing OTBR launch sequence releases H2 BOOT, asserts reset for 10 ms, releases it, waits 100 ms and only then starts the OpenThread/Spinel stack. That fresh H2 boot clears prior parser state. Keep logging on native USB, preserve this ordering and verify warm S3 restart as well as simultaneous cold start. There is no need to burn eFuses just to suppress early bytes.
- **GPIO48:** the onboard SCK indicator is driven through a 2N7002 gate with a 976 kΩ pull-down. This is a capacitive load, not an LED directly drawing GPIO current. Prefer a slow control output there; check its default-low state against the selected peripheral. It is not a reason to reject the board.
- **GPIO0/46:** these also reach RGB circuitry. Keep them out of the normal peripheral map. GPIO0 remains available for the service boot strap.

These findings come from the [Nano schematic](https://files.waveshare.com/wiki/ESP32-S3-Nano/ESP32-S3-Nano-Schematic.pdf) and [current OTBR launch code](../../../../firmware/low-power/components/low_power_otbr/low_power_otbr.cpp). The current code's reset ordering is evidence for a design choice, not a substitute for testing the final Nano image.

## Lower-change alternative: Mini pin audit

The manufacturer schematic exposes 27 GPIO on four eight-position through-hole rows: GPIO1–18, 21, 33–38, 43 and 44. Excluding strap GPIO3 and reserving GPIO43/44 for ROM UART leaves **exactly 24 application GPIO**. Native USB GPIO19/20, BOOT GPIO0 and the GPIO47 RGB LED remain separate. The board is specifically **ESP32-S3FH4R2, 4 MB flash and 2 MB Quad PSRAM**. Do not substitute an octal-PSRAM variant and assume GPIO33–37 remain available. [LOLIN schematic](https://www.wemos.cc/en/latest/_static/files/sch_s3_mini_v1.0.0.pdf), [manufacturer product specification](https://www.wemos.cc/en/latest/s3/s3_mini.html)

An independent check of [pin-plan-mini.json](pin-plan-mini.json) matched all 24 assigned GPIO to the manufacturer's header numbers, found no duplicates, and found no collision with reserved pins. Only the following application connections move:

- Thread host TX/RX: GPIO41/42 → GPIO33/34.
- H2 reset/BOOT controls: GPIO47/48 → GPIO37/38, retaining open-drain operation and their existing idle states.
- Radar TX/RX: GPIO39/40 → GPIO35/36.

Ethernet, CC1101, the four-wire Ember UART and I²C remain on their current GPIO. The H2 side remains GPIO4/RX, GPIO5/TX, EN and GPIO9/BOOT. The XIAO side remains PC07/RX, PC06/TX, PC04/CTS and PC05/RTS. UART baud rates, TCP endpoints and the external radio firmware need not change merely because the S3 board changes. The pin plan preserves interfaces; it is not itself an end-to-end HA test.

The H2 currently runs a Thread RCP; the MG24 runs an Ember NCP. Neither existing image supplies the proposed general Ethernet forwarding service. Adding W5500 to either requires another transport protocol, queues, timeout handling and a reset/recovery contract, while the S3 still owns BLE proxying, the border-router stack and other host services. It also changes radio firmware that currently has a narrow job. A smaller S3 preserves these boundaries. See the existing [MC1 firmware contract](../../../../firmware/module-carrier/README.md) and [Ember build](../../../../firmware/module-carrier/zigbee/README.md).

## Physical savings and ordinary soldering

The **43.18 × 17.78 mm Nano** occupies 767.74 mm², versus 1,593.60 mm² for the current 62.74 × 25.4 mm DevKit body: a **51.82% reduction in the S3 board's area**. The **34.3 × 25.4 mm Mini** occupies 871.22 mm², a 45.33% reduction; it is shorter but wider than Nano. These are module-area calculations, **not carrier-area predictions**. The active carrier is 100 × 90 mm and the alternative square study is 100 × 100 mm. Their other radios, RJ45 exit, support points, antennas and service envelopes still have to fit after substitution. [Nano dimensions](https://docs.waveshare.com/ESP32-S3-Nano#dimensions), [LOLIN dimensional drawing](https://www.wemos.cc/en/latest/_static/files/dim_s3_mini_v1.0.0.pdf), [active geometry](../../cad/design.json)

All required Nano and Mini signals are on plated through-holes. Ordinary male pins can face down through the carrier with no underside signal wires or castellated-edge soldering. Nano-M is the header version, listed as SKU26752. Confirm the actual supplied pins and their direction. For Mini, specifically confirm all four factory-soldered rows; a bare-board listing is not evidence of a preassembled module. If headers are supplied loose, fitting them is ordinary through-hole work but still adds a step relative to the requested factory-pin assembly.

The four-row layout is denser than a two-row DevKit. The new footprint must retain access to the carrier's bottom solder joints, adequate pin length, clearance under the module for its existing components, and room to install the module squarely before soldering. Include the real USB plug, BOOT/RESET access and harness housings in the placement check. Do not infer those envelopes from the rectangular PCB outline. Removing the USB connector would not shorten the Mini PCB.

## Service and power are board-specific

**Nano:** native USB-C remains available, but only RESET has an onboard button. Provide accessible GPIO0-to-GND boot entry; do not bury the B1/GPIO0 pin after direct soldering. The proposed use of GPIO43 for H2 removes the idea of a completely unconnected UART-TX service pin: external UART service must account for the attached H2, its power and reset state. Neither Nano nor Mini contains the old DevKit's onboard USB-UART bridge. Losing that second built-in USB path is a symmetric service-hardware tradeoff, not a special defect of Mini.

Nano's USB VBUS and header VUSB are common. Onboard D3 conducts **VUSB → VIN**, and VIN feeds the MP2322 buck regulator. That diode protects the USB side from VIN; it does not automatically isolate the carrier's common supply from a USB-powered Nano. Keep branch isolation. The vendor lists 6–21 V for external VIN while its USB path naturally supplies the regulator through a diode from 5 V. The reviewed proposal feeds existing isolated 5 V through the existing carrier branch Schottky into **JP1.15 VIN**, leaving JP1.12 VUSB and JP1.2 local 3.3 V unconnected on the carrier. This and onboard USB D3 form two-source diode OR-ing without adding a part or putting both diodes in series with the carrier supply. A chip-level operating range alone is not a board-level qualification. Confirm voltage drop, startup, loaded 3.3 V and USB backfeed with the chosen scheme. [Nano power schematic](https://files.waveshare.com/wiki/ESP32-S3-Nano/ESP32-S3-Nano-Schematic.pdf), [vendor supply specification](https://docs.waveshare.com/ESP32-S3-Nano#product-parameter-comparison), [MP2322 manufacturer](https://www.monolithicpower.com/en/mp2322.html)

The Mini has one native USB-C interface rather than the DevKit's native-USB plus USB-UART pair. Native USB remains the normal programming/recovery path. Reserved GPIO43/44 keep ROM UART available electrically, but that fallback requires an external USB-to-UART adapter and accessible BOOT/EN controls. Thus the proposal preserves a recovery route, **not identical onboard service hardware**.

In the Mini schematic, USB VBUS and header VBUS are the same net feeding the ME6211C33 regulator. No input OR-ing diode is shown. Keep the carrier branch isolation and define how host VBUS is isolated during externally powered Mini service; do not directly parallel supplies or assume a USB cable is data-only. Verify enumeration with the intended data/power arrangement, rail voltages, and peer back-power behaviour before publishing a service instruction. Do not generalise this to XIAO: its SAMD11 probe needs USB VBUS even when its header VIN is already powered. [LOLIN schematic](https://www.wemos.cc/en/latest/_static/files/sch_s3_mini_v1.0.0.pdf), [XIAO service evidence](../square-layout/service-review.md)

The smaller board does not by itself remove the carrier's three isolation diodes, two RF pull-ups, or WK2132 UART expansion. Those parts address supply topology, reset states and the extra serial port; a change in S3 dimensions does not eliminate their functions. Likewise, do not assume the Mini regulator can supply every peripheral just because it exposes 3.3 V. Measure the selected supply arrangement under simultaneous radio and Ethernet load.

## Memory and alternative boards

The existing 8 MB-flash build uses an approximately 1.717 MB application image and 3,932,160-byte OTA application slots. Nano's 16 MB flash and 8 MB PSRAM preserve or increase those capacities. Mini cannot reuse those OTA slots: it requires a full build with 4 MB partitions and 2 MB **Quad** PSRAM, followed by verification that both OTA slots fit the resulting image. A successful Mini build would prove fit, not equivalence to 8 MB RAM. For either board, measure minimum internal heap, largest free block, stack margins and PSRAM use during concurrent BLE connections, Thread traffic, Ember traffic, Z-Wave UART service, RF activity and an OTA cycle. [Baseline build evidence](../../../../firmware/module-carrier/build-evidence.json)

Waveshare S3 Zero's advertised total GPIO count includes contacts unsuitable for the requested header-only assembly: the reviewed header scheme provides only 15 GPIO. It therefore cannot carry this 24-pin design, or even the 21-pin shared-SPI variant, without additional underside connections or expansion. The newer N8R8 SKU does not by its name establish a different usable header count. [Zero documentation and SKU list](https://docs.waveshare.com/ESP32-S3-Zero), [Zero schematic](https://files.waveshare.com/wiki/ESP32-S3-Zero/ESP32-S3-Zero-Sch.pdf)

## Shared SPI: acceptable change with explicit reset handling

ESPHome 2026.9.1 has a W5500 `spi_id` path, so sharing is technically possible. Both devices can release MISO: W5500 specifies high impedance while deselected, and the inspected CC1101 driver sets IOCFG1 to 0x2E. CC1101's raw RF path uses the GDO pins/RMT, rather than moving every pulse across SPI. [W5500 datasheet](https://docs.wiznet.io/img/products/w5500/W5500_ds_v110e.pdf), [CC1101 datasheet, sections 10 and 26](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/cc1101.pdf)

However, the pinned CC1101 driver's manual reset sequence toggles CS and leaves it low for 5 ms outside the SPI lock. Current startup ordering runs CC1101 setup at DATA/600 before Ethernet at 250, and the project's RF package does not call runtime reset/configure. This explains why that initial sequence is not presently concurrent with Ethernet; it does **not** make arbitrary runtime reset safe on a shared bus. The current study retains that fixed startup/no-runtime-reset constraint; it does **not** patch the CC1101 reset implementation. Guard the constraint with a source regression check and test latency under simultaneous load. Any later runtime-reset/reconfigure feature must first lock the complete reset sequence correctly. Normal transfer locking alone is insufficient proof. This is a bounded software constraint consistent with the user's acceptance of firmware work to keep hardware simple. It does not justify reducing memory capacity merely to avoid changing bus topology. [Pinned CC1101 driver](https://github.com/esphome/esphome/blob/2026.9.1/esphome/components/cc1101/cc1101.cpp), [SPI bus locking](https://github.com/esphome/esphome/blob/2026.9.1/esphome/components/spi/spi_esp_idf.cpp), [Ethernet priority](https://github.com/esphome/esphome/blob/2026.9.1/esphome/components/ethernet/ethernet_component.h)

## Antennas and the ceiling

Place the Nano's ceramic antenna or Mini's printed antenna at an outer carrier edge, with the appropriate copper/track/via exclusion and no permanent wiring or mounting metal nearby. Also preserve independent H2 and XIAO antenna clearances. A smaller S3 is an opportunity to move the square study's internal H2 toward an edge; it is not permission to pack three antenna ends together. Apply the actual module antenna geometry, rather than copying a generic rectangular keepout.

Ceiling mounting with the populated side facing the room does not eliminate near-field interaction with carrier copper only millimetres behind an antenna. Preserve the intended ground under circuitry and the RF feed, while excluding copper from the radiating-end region. Moving the entire assembly away from the ceiling does not increase module-to-carrier separation. Espressif favours an antenna beyond the baseboard edge and explicitly advises against centred placement with a hole around all four sides. Final ceiling/enclosure orientation, range and simultaneous-radio behaviour remain measured acceptance items; no equal-range or dB claim is established by this study. [Espressif placement guidance](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32s3/pcb-layout-design.html#general-principles-of-pcb-layout-for-modules-positioning-a-module-on-a-base-board), [ceiling-specific explanation](../square-layout/usb-rf-review.md#ceiling-mounting-and-copper-beneath-the-antenna)

## Implementation gates

1. Confirm the exact Nano-M revision, memory, male-header assembly and power pin polarity against the purchased unit. Retain Mini as a documented reduced-memory alternative rather than silently treating it as capacity-equivalent.
2. Preserve the audited 21-pin Nano map, enforce shared-SPI reset constraints and preserve the H2 reset-before-Spinel ordering. Compile the complete application and record image/partition sizes separately from runtime claims.
3. Produce a new native carrier placement with antenna, USB, harness and underside soldering envelopes before choosing a board dimension. Pass native ERC/DRC and an independent net/pad audit; the existing carriers' passes do not transfer to it.
4. Test native-USB recovery, external ROM-UART fallback, OTA, power sequencing and all HA interfaces under simultaneous load. Keep the active design available until these checks establish practical parity.

**Independent evidence at this revision:** manufacturer drawings and source inspected; all 24 Mini and 21 proposed Nano GPIO allocations checked against header assignments; shared-SPI startup/reset and H2 reset-before-Spinel behaviour inspected. No device was flashed, no new carrier was reviewed, and no runtime, thermal, power or RF measurements were performed by this reviewer. Build results are tracked separately by the firmware study; this report does not turn compilation into hardware acceptance.
