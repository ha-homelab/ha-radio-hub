# Square-layout study: USB service and antenna review

**17 October 2026. Study only; no active CAD or firmware was changed.** The 100 × 100 mm candidate can retain the same runtime interfaces. Keep the factory USB connectors where practical: removing them is not necessary for runtime operation, but it does not automatically preserve recovery or remove the need for a service connection.

## What the connectors actually do

**S3:** the specified official ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 v1.1 N8R8 has **two Micro-USB connectors**, not USB-C. One reaches the onboard USB-to-UART bridge; the other reaches native USB on GPIO19/D− and GPIO20/D+, supporting programming and USB debugging. BOOT is GPIO0, reset is EN. A board sold with USB-C connectors needs its actual revision and dimensions checked before reusing this footprint. [Espressif board guide](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-dev-kits/en/latest/esp32s3/esp32-s3-devkitc-1/user_guide_v1.1.html)

**H2:** the official DevKitM-1 has two USB-C connectors: the USB-UART bridge and native USB on GPIO26/D− and GPIO27/D+. ROM UART service uses GPIO23/RX and GPIO24/TX, with GPIO9/BOOT and EN. MC1 deliberately puts the running Thread RCP UART on GPIO4/5, leaving the factory service ports available. The existing S3 firmware controls H2 reset/boot but **does not flash H2**. Removing the ports does not turn the running RCP UART into a ROM recovery port. [Espressif H2 guide](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-dev-kits/en/latest/esp32h2/esp32-h2-devkitm-1/user_guide.html), [MC1 firmware contract](../../../../firmware/module-carrier/README.md)

**XIAO MG24:** USB-C reaches its onboard **SAMD11 CMSIS-DAP/debug and serial bridge**. In MC1, the Ember NCP uses separate PC06/PC07/PC04/PC05 header pins; removing USB therefore need not stop normal Zigbee operation. J8/PC00 plus reset can enter the custom UART bootloader, but this depends on an intact, correctly installed bootloader. It cannot replace SWD recovery after an erased or damaged bootloader. The factory USB programmer is the existing independent recovery path. [MC1 XIAO firmware](../../../../firmware/module-carrier/zigbee/README.md), [Seeed recovery instructions](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/xiao_mg24_getting_started/#unbricking)

The [Seeed native CAD](https://files.seeedstudio.com/wiki/XIAO_MG24/Getting_Start/XIAO_MG24_v1.0_KiCad_260114.zip) confirms USB1 A6/B6 = `SAMD11_USB_DP` and A7/B7 = `SAMD11_USB_DN`; those small USB pads are not ordinary header pins. The alternative MG24 debug points are **TP17/SWDIO, TP18/SWCLK and TP4/reset**, all **1.143 mm bottom-side test pads**. A permanent carrier can obstruct them. External SWD would need planned access, power/ground and control of the onboard probe, not a presumed spare header.

## Removal versus loss of access

A USB connector body, its inserted plug, and the cable's bend envelope are three different volumes. Desoldering the connector removes its body; keeping an unused connector removes only the need for a permanently inserted cable. Neither choice shortens the development-board PCB. The current **30 mm service allowance is an engineering envelope**, not a 30 mm-long onboard socket.

- **Runtime parity:** possible without USB sockets; Ethernet, Thread RCP and four-wire Ember do not require a USB host cable.
- **Existing service parity:** not preserved by simply removing or obstructing every port. OTA depends on working S3 software, and XIAO UART recovery depends on its bootloader.
- **Recovery with replacement wiring:** feasible, but it adds a documented service interface and validation. S3/H2 native USB pins are available at module headers but presently unconnected on MC1. USB relocation requires a short suitable differential path and strain relief. External UART also needs care: removing the socket leaves the onboard USB-UART chip electrically connected and does not guarantee its TX output is isolated.

If rework is chosen, program and verify recovery before permanent mounting and preserve accessible reset/boot controls. **Do not apply a blanket USB VBUS-blocker rule to XIAO:** its SAMD11 regulator is supplied and enabled by USB VBUS, while the carrier powers the header VIN behind the onboard diode. The probe therefore needs USB VBUS; normal header power is not its intended supply. Keep the complete carrier powered during assembled XIAO service, qualify the diode-isolated rails and USB enumeration, and avoid powering XIAO alone through signal-connected peers. S3/H2 need their own port-specific power checks. The [service review](service-review.md#xiaos-programming-probe-needs-usb-vbus) documents the netlist evidence and this correction to earlier MC1 guidance. Connector removal is an authorized option in this study; it remains additional rework.

## Reviewed candidate geometry

Final study anchors are S3 **(74,3), 0°**; H2 **(62,52), 180°**; XIAO **(18,59), 270°**; WIZ850io **(60.7,92.9), 180°**. Coordinates are millimetres in the proposed carrier frame. Independent parsing of the generated candidate matched all **145 native pad entries, positions and assigned nets** and found no overlaps in the declared header-mating, USB-service and 5 mm support envelopes. This is an unrouted placement study, not a fabrication or RF pass.

XIAO at 270° has its **ceramic antenna toward the left outer edge** and USB toward the centre. Its PCB occupies approximately **x=−0.034…20.921, y=57.73…75.51**. The retained 14 × 30 mm USB allowance is **x=20.921…50.921, y=59.62…73.62**. It sits between H2's antenna end at **58.975** and WIZ's PCB start at **74.3**, leaving only **0.645/0.680 mm** to those physical outlines and **0.180 mm** to WIZ's courtyard. These are tight clearances for the exact slim-plug dry fit, not generous cable clearance. The earlier XIAO y=52 candidate intersected the H2 board and antenna; moving it to y=59 corrects that conflict.

The plug envelope still enters the **larger H2 RF clearance region**. It is therefore a temporary maintenance condition, not a route for a permanently attached USB pigtail or the configuration in which normal RF performance should be measured. Preserve the intended upward cable exit after insertion. WIZ's opening faces downward at **y=102 mm**, 2 mm beyond the square's bottom edge; S3 USB exits downward and H2 USB upward. Actual plug bodies, insertion paths and harness bends remain to be checked.

## Antenna constraints

XIAO uses an actual **ceramic antenna**, selected by MC1 firmware with PB04 low/PB05 high. The specified S3 and H2 boards use **printed PCB antennas**. Removing USB connectors changes none of these antenna footprints. At XIAO origin (18,y), rotation270°, the ceramic centre is approximately **(0.9661, y+11.2996)**. The candidate exclusion **x=0…5, y=64…73.1** covers the projected ceramic body; keep its left-edge surroundings clear.

The inverted H2 antenna remains **inside** the proposed square carrier, unlike an antenna extending beyond the outside edge. Its separation from WIZ's PCB is **15.325 mm**, and the S3 board is approximately **15.56 mm** laterally from its antenna edge. Those are useful geometric margins, but do not by themselves qualify the placement. The candidate reserves **x=28.9…72.3, y=53.5…74** against carrier copper, tracks and vias, extending about 15 mm laterally and forward from the antenna. Keep permanent headers, harnesses and metal out of this region. Any cutback should be assessed against the edge-placement guidance below; an isolated internal hole is not a qualified remedy. Do not route a permanently attached XIAO USB cable across it.

Espressif recommends putting PCB antennas outside the baseboard, or keeping the feed point near an edge and cutting back the baseboard beside and beneath the antenna. The guide explicitly advises against a module centred on the board with clearance hollowed out on all four sides. It also recommends at least 15 mm clearance within the enclosure. Neither a central copper-free patch nor a small isolated internal cutout is equivalent to the preferred edge placement. The square arrangement therefore needs a deliberate RF compromise documented and measured, including simultaneous Thread, BLE and Zigbee traffic. [H2 layout guidance](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32h2/pcb-layout-design.html), [S3 layout guidance](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32s3/pcb-layout-design.html)

## Ceiling mounting and copper beneath the antenna

Assume the populated side faces the room, with the carrier between the modules and the ceiling; the ceiling construction is not yet established. The open room is favourable for propagation, but it does not remove the antenna's interaction with material only millimetres away. Here **beneath the antenna** means the carrier area directly opposite its radiating element, regardless of which way the assembly faces in the room.

A nearby copper sheet supports induced RF currents and changes the antenna's local electric and magnetic fields. That can shift its resonance and feed impedance, alter the radiation pattern and reduce useful radiated or received power. This happens in the **near field**, before considering the path across the room. The antenna and its intended ground geometry form one RF structure; a new sheet immediately behind the radiating end changes that structure. Open space in front does not undo the change. TI's antenna note explains both proximity detuning and the importance of the ground-plane position. [TI AN-1811, sections 2–3](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/snoa519b/snoa519b.pdf)

These PCB and ceramic antennas do not send a single beam perpendicular to their board face. They have three-dimensional radiation patterns and polarisation; mounting face-down does not make everything behind them irrelevant. This is also why the instruction is **not to remove ground under the whole radio**: preserve the designed ground/return structure under the circuitry and RF feed, while respecting the specific copper-free radiating-end region. A deliberately designed antenna with a ground reflector is a different geometry from adding copper behind these existing modules.

A nonmetallic ceiling offset can reduce interaction with the ceiling and any nearby metal, but moving the entire assembly away from the ceiling leaves the module-to-carrier spacing unchanged. It cannot cure copper immediately behind the antenna. Use nonmetallic supports, keep mounting metal and permanent cables out of the antenna clearances, and assess the final enclosure and installed orientation. The square candidate's central H2 remains an RF compromise even when mounted on the ceiling; prefer an outer-edge antenna placement if revising it, or validate the compromise experimentally. No specific loss in dB or universal ceiling offset is established here. [Espressif H2 placement and enclosure guidance](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32h2/pcb-layout-design.html#general-principles-of-pcb-layout-for-modules-positioning-a-module-on-a-base-board)

**Recommendation:** retain the factory connectors in the corrected y=59 study, with accessible buttons and explicit H2 RF space. Verify the actual slim USB plug and upward cable routing before adopting the placement. If full service access still does not fit, change placement before deleting the XIAO recovery connection. A desoldered-port variant can preserve runtime functions, but needs a separately specified and tested recovery path before claiming full parity.
