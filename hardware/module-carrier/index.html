The selected architecture

The former C0/M0, I0 and D0 paths are historical references. The all-H2 experiment was not selected because its local border-router implementation and Zigbee integration/backup behavior did not meet the compatibility priority.

The compact PCB

The carrier is 80 × 90 mm, with 20.0% less PCB area than the preceding 100 × 90 mm carrier and 55.0% less than the original 160 × 100 mm outline. Harness headers occupy internal gaps. The full-size S3 is replaced with a factory-header Nano-M; the H2, MG24 and WIZ850io roles remain. GPIO assignments and the shared SPI bus are updated together in CAD and firmware. Cable/service reservations are shown on the assembly drawing and are not usable component space.

Both dimensions stay within the 100 × 100 mm size ceiling used by advertised low-cost prototype offers. All components remain on top; the underside carries copper and solder joints. A smaller outline does not imply a proportional reduction in the delivered fabrication price. Cost rationale and source offers; shipping and final fabrication specifications need their own quote.

RJ45 faces outward at the bottom edge; its mouth projects 2 mm beyond the carrier. Keep the marked plug and latch corridor clear. Use the new Nano pin map and matching firmware.

What changed after the SMLIGHT review

The host and two independent radios remain. W5500 and CC1101 share one SPI bus with separate chip selects, freeing three host pins. No extra processor, GPIO expander or internal Ethernet hop is introduced. The smaller Nano retains 16 MB flash and 8 MB PSRAM. Source review · Host selection history.

Direct solder mounting

  1. Match exact module revisions to the drawings. Program each module before permanent assembly.
  2. Use the factory-header ESP32-S3-Nano-M. Its JP2 numbering runs opposite to the visual row order; follow physical pad numbers, not an assumed left-to-right sequence.
  3. Dry-fit the male pins in the plated holes. Check the compact layout against actual module bodies, plugged USB/RJ45 connectors, vertical harness plugs and antenna keepouts.
  4. Fit the listed through-hole parts, then solder module pins from the underside. No female module sockets.
  5. Wire the finished isolated power and UART modules and the existing radios/sensors to the labeled carrier connectors.
  6. Feed the Nano through the carrier diode to VIN, leaving Nano VBUS and 3V3 disconnected from carrier rails. Confirm loaded VIN and 3V3, including concurrent radio activity. Keep the GPIO0 recovery contacts accessible.
  7. Check polarity, continuity and loaded rail voltages before connecting the full assembly.
  8. For later H2 USB programming, activate Pause Thread for H2 Service, then verify maintenance state and Thread Guard Durable before flashing. Explicit retry resumes normal operation.
  9. Commission one function at a time, then test all functions together including power loss and recovery.
Power and service: separate the controller supply paths from the peripheral 3.3 V rail. Do not parallel regulator outputs. Carrier diode drops require loaded-rail checks. Nano receives the diode-isolated 5 V branch at VIN; VBUS and local 3V3 remain isolated. This is below its documented external VIN range and needs a loaded-rail test. USB service needs a board-specific power procedure; merely unplugging PoE can leave back-power paths through signal pins.

Still requires the real assembly

  • Nano VIN operation below the documented external 6 V minimum, including branch-diode loss and simultaneous-load transients
  • USB/back-power isolation in the real assembly
  • Mechanical fit and antenna spacing
  • RF coexistence and range
  • End-to-end ZHA / Thread / Z-Wave commissioning
  • Simultaneous workload reliability

One current layout reduces the choice and assembly burden. Board-specific firmware configuration and UART-transport hardening remain real work; no claim of zero software changes or guaranteed lower power is made.
