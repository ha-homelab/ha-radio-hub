The selected architecture

The former C0/M0, I0 and D0 paths are historical references. The all-H2 experiment was not selected because its local border-router implementation and Zigbee integration/backup behavior did not meet the compatibility priority.

The compact PCB

The carrier fits within 47.5 × 75 mm, with 26.9% less PCB area than the historical 65 × 75 mm single-sided carrier and 77.7% less than the original 160 × 100 mm outline. S3, H2 and MG24 face the room. WIZ850io, all seven male harness headers, three diodes and two resistors face the ceiling. This permits selected opposite-side overlap; plated holes, solder tails, underside parts and antenna exclusions still require clearance. USB cable corridors remain absent. Program and verify each controller before soldering. Later updates use the network paths described below.

Both dimensions stay within the 100 × 100 mm size ceiling used by advertised low-cost prototype offers. Component placement now uses both sides of the same two-layer PCB. Two-sided manual soldering needs an explicit assembly order; it does not require four copper layers. A smaller outline does not imply a proportional reduction in the delivered fabrication price. Cost rationale and source offers; shipping and final fabrication specifications need their own quote.

RJ45 faces outward at the bottom edge; its mouth projects 2 mm beyond the carrier. The Ethernet module is on the ceiling-facing side. Keep its plug and latch corridor clear. The electrical pin map and matching firmware are unchanged from the 65 × 75 mm revision.

One retention hole, supported edges

The 47.5 × 75 mm PCB retains only H3 between U2 and U3. Split enclosure rails support both long edges; H3 locates and retains the board. Removing the other three holes and moving the controllers inward reduces area by 6.9% from the preceding 51 × 75 mm board. Two copper layers, sixteen fitted electronic items and every pin assignment are retained.

Rail study, assembly sequence and printable fit coupons. The groove must have measured clearance; it is not a zero-clearance press fit.

Placement and routed length

The entire rear is soldered first: WIZ, all seven male harness headers and the five axial parts. Front U1–U3 are fitted afterwards and soldered from the accessible rear. Opposite-side overlap is allowed; every remaining solder land and the measured height clearance must pass. D1 and R1 retain useful diagonal orientations, while S3 and H2 are brought closer together. Pin assignments, polarity and track-width rules are unchanged.

What changed after the SMLIGHT review

The host and two independent radios remain. W5500 and CC1101 share one SPI bus with separate chip selects, freeing three host pins. No extra processor, GPIO expander or internal Ethernet hop is introduced. The smaller Nano retains 16 MB flash and 8 MB PSRAM. Source review · Host selection history.

Programming without USB service gaps

Initial programming is performed before the modules are soldered. Subsequent S3 updates use ESPHome OTA; H2 updates pass through the S3 to its ROM UART; MG24 application updates use its existing Ember bootloader and serial bridge. See the firmware instructions and tested limits.

Required H2 preparation: on the specified DevKitM-1 v1.3, remove R7 (470 Ω) before assembly to isolate the USB-UART transmitter. The carrier now uses H2 RX23 / TX24. Verify the actual board revision and load the matching RCP image; the former RX4 / TX5 image is incompatible.

Printable carrier enclosure

The current package contains a ceiling-side base, a room-side retaining cover, a separate jack-stop trial and a 4 mm ceiling spacing frame. The PCB is held by the legal edge lands and the single H3 hole. Case closure and ceiling screws are separate from the PCB mounting.

84.3 × 121.8 × 57.4 mm trial outer envelope; 61.4 mm installed depth. Install and adjust floor hardware on the bench before mounting the base to the ceiling. The spacing frame accommodates underside screw heads up to 3 mm. The 26 mm rear and 25 mm front cavities are explicit assumptions, not measured module heights. This houses the carrier only; the PoE splitter, regulator, UART bridge, extra radios and sensors remain outside it.

Two-sided direct solder mounting

Follow the assembly and height review: solder the complete rear first, then fit and solder the front controllers. Stock header height is not assumed to make opposing pins safe: measure the assembled gap and trim only while preserving sound through-hole solder joints.

  1. Identify the exact modules and male header geometry; pre-program and test every controller before soldering.
  2. On H2 DevKitM-1 v1.3, remove R7 and verify USB-UART TX isolation. Retain the matching RX23/TX24 image.
  3. Temporarily wire all functions and network updates. The two-sided relocation does not change the pin map or firmware.
  4. Dry-fit both faces and measure factory spacer gaps, underside parts, opposing tail heights and ceiling-facing plug clearance.
  5. Complete the entire ceiling-facing back first: WIZ850io, J1–J7, D1–D3 and R1–R2. Solder from the front, then trim, inspect and electrically check all joints while visible. Leave J8/J9 unpopulated.
  6. Only then fit front U1–U3 and solder their pins from the rear. Every front-module solder land must remain accessible with the rear already populated. Deep opposite-side overlap is permitted after the access and measured-clearance checks.
  7. Inspect and test every rail and net for continuity and unwanted contact, including after gentle connector loading. Check loaded Nano VIN and 3V3.
  8. Print the rail coupons and measure the populated stack first. On the bench, fit and adjust the base floor hardware and the separate jack stop; underside screw-head projection must be at most 3 mm. Install the 4 mm ceiling spacing frame before mounting the base. Load the PCB normally into the open edge supports, retain it at H3, then close the cover against its hard stops. Provide cable restraint without loading the WIZ header joints.
  9. Commission all services together and verify network updates, interrupted updates, power cycling, thermal behavior and RF range before mounting permanently.
Power and assembly: separate the controller supply paths from the peripheral 3.3 V rail. Do not parallel regulator outputs. Carrier diode drops require loaded-rail checks. Nano receives the diode-isolated 5 V branch at VIN; VBUS and local 3V3 remain isolated. This is below its documented external VIN range and needs a loaded-rail test. Complete individual USB programming and checks before soldering; the assembled layout reserves no USB insertion space.

Still requires the real assembly

  • Factory pin spacing in Z, trimmed solder-tail height and opposing underside-component clearance
  • Soldering access and practical assembly order on purchased module variants
  • Printed rail fit, actual solder growth, jack-load support, housing thermal creep and fit of the generated full enclosure
  • Loaded Nano VIN operation below the documented external 6 V minimum
  • H2 R7 isolation, boot straps and network flashing on actual hardware
  • MG24 update interruption and recovery on actual hardware
  • RF coexistence, ceiling clearance, range and simultaneous workload reliability
  • End-to-end commissioning and network backup/restore

One current layout reduces the choice and assembly burden. Board-specific firmware configuration and UART-transport hardening remain real work; no claim of zero software changes or guaranteed lower power is made.
